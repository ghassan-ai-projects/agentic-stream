## Deeper workflow, adoption, and economic evidence (2026-09-24)

These sources were added after the first-pass review. Vendor pages describe published product capability or pricing mechanics, not comparative results. Public case studies and SEC filings are identified as self-reported evidence.

### S27. NIST MEP pump downtime case

[Invest Time to Reduce Down Time](https://www.nist.gov/mep/successstories/2023/invest-time-reduce-down-time), National Institute of Standards and Technology Manufacturing Extension Partnership, 2023. A 50-employee Kennametal site had recurring processing-pump failures. The intervention included lean process work, a mandatory lockout/tagout form, pre-removal checklist, numerical tracking of failing pumps, root-cause analysis, and targeted preventive maintenance. The site had set a 75% downtime-reduction goal, but the page does not report the measured percentage. It reports a $6,000 workforce-development investment, cost savings in parts/labor, decreased waste/lost time, three employee ideas, and improved maintenance traceability. This is a single, non-controlled success story and a counterexample to assuming that an agent runtime is necessary.

### S28. NIST maintenance-economics evidence limit

[The Costs and Benefits of Advanced Maintenance in Manufacturing](https://www.nist.gov/publications/costs-and-benefits-advanced-maintenance-manufacturing), NIST Advanced Manufacturing Series 100-18, 2018. The report says public data and literature do not support precise national maintenance-loss or advanced-maintenance benefit estimates without strong assumptions that introduce substantial unmeasurable error. It says manufacturers may be able to provide estimates, but the needed costs and losses often are not separately recorded. This is a reason to use site-level records and customer-supplied loss accounting, not a market-size or ROI estimate.

### S29. SRE alerting and live-incident practice

[Monitoring Distributed Systems](https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/) and [Managing Incidents](https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/), Google SRE Book. The former describes actionable, high-signal/low-noise alerts and the cost of interrupting humans; the latter describes incident roles, a living incident state document, communications, and explicit handoffs. These are mature practitioner methods and reinforce that incident handling includes human coordination and recordkeeping, not only event correlation.

### S30. Prometheus Alertmanager primitives

[Alertmanager concepts](https://prometheus.io/docs/alerting/0.34/alertmanager/), Prometheus documentation. Alertmanager groups, deduplicates, routes, silences, and inhibits alerts. It is a credible, low-cost baseline for simple alert-noise and routing problems; it does not establish the limits of a customer's full incident stack.

### S34. AWS SiteWise native anomaly detection

[Native anomaly detection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/iot-sitewise/latest/userguide/sitewise-anomaly-detection.html), [best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/iot-sitewise/latest/userguide/ano-best-practices.html), [advanced inference configurations](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/iot-sitewise/latest/userguide/advanced-inference-configurations.html), [reviewing inference results](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/iot-sitewise/latest/userguide/reviewing-inference-results.html), and [pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/iot-sitewise/pricing/), AWS documentation, accessed 2026-09-24. SiteWise documents native anomaly detection for fixed, stationary equipment including pumps and motors. Current guidance recommends at least 14 days of data spanning normal operating modes and says the feature does not support input data below 1 Hz. For inference it also provides a configurable data-delay offset of 0–60 minutes to include late-arriving readings; that is a real late-data alternative to test, though its relationship to post-publication version correction and pending-action revalidation is different and must be evaluated on a concrete trace. SiteWise Edge separately meters data processing at $200 per active gateway/month on the current pricing page, plus other service dimensions. Its older alarm path uses AWS IoT Events, whose support ended in May 2026 (see S23); do not treat that alarm path as a viable new-deployment baseline. The native model and current user's data rate, alarm/CMMS path and eligibility must be checked in any pilot.

### S37. Industrial-maintenance purchasing proxy

[MultiSensor AI FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1863990/000110465926048689/msai-20251231xars.pdf), filed 2026.
