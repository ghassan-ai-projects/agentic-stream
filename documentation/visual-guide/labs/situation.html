Lab 2 · Stream plane

A condition changes state, not its mind every second

A noisy sensor crosses a threshold again and again. A Situation should not flap with it. Compare a naive single threshold with a spec-style rule (separate enter and exit thresholds, so the line it must cross to leave is not the line it crossed to enter, plus a minimum duration), and see why every change becomes an immutable, numbered version.

Reading the picture

Two machines, one signal

One synthetic signal drives both state machines. The strips under the chart show each machine's state over time and count its flips. Each flip of the spec machine publishes a version into the stack.

Key idea

Hysteresis and minimum duration

Enter above 30 °C, but only leave at or below 28 °C, and only believe a new state after it has held for a minimum duration. Small wobbles stop mattering. This lab applies the hold to leaving as well as entering, as the spec's rules do.

Why it matters

Versions are facts, not drafts

A published version never changes. A correction publishes a new one, so you can always reconstruct what the system believed at the time, and every later step can point at exactly that version.

Words used here

Hysteresis
Using one threshold to enter a state and a different one to leave it, so a signal hovering near a line cannot flip the state back and forth.
Minimum duration
How long a condition must keep holding before the state changes. A brief spike does not count.
quiet, pending, open, recovering
The lab's four states. The spec's own phases are finer: candidate, watch, cooling, recovering, resolved. Quiet is "no occurrence". Pending is the candidate phase waiting out its 60 s hold. Open stands for watch and cooling. Recovering waits 120 s at or below the exit threshold before resolving.
Provisional, on-time
Each window step publishes an early (provisional) version, then an on-time one when the window closes.
The signal is synthetic, for illustration. The 30 °C enter and 28 °C exit thresholds and the holds (60 s to watch, 30 s to cooling, 120 s to resolve) come from the recorded run's spec.
